    How this calculator works

    We first work out your standard monthly payment from the balance, rate and years left:

    Payment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n)

    P is the balance, r the monthly interest rate and n the months left. Each month, interest is charged on the remaining balance, and the rest of your payment, plus any extra, reduces the balance. Paying extra means less balance, so less interest next month, and the effect compounds.

    Assumptions: a fixed rate for the whole period, payments at month end, no fees or early-repayment charges. Sources and reviewer details will appear here at launch.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is it better to overpay my mortgage or save?

    If your mortgage rate is higher than what your savings earn after tax, overpaying usually saves more. Keep an emergency fund first, and check whether your lender charges for early repayment.

    Do extra payments reduce my monthly payment?

    Usually no: most lenders keep the payment the same and shorten the term, which saves the most interest. Some let you choose to lower the payment instead.

    Is a lump sum or a monthly extra better?

    Money paid earlier saves more interest, so a lump sum now beats the same total spread over years. Monthly extras are easier to sustain.

    Is my data stored?

    No. Everything is calculated in your browser. Saved plans stay on this device only.
